# Fact Verification Casebook

Short fact-checking write-ups on widely repeated claims. For each claim I check independent sources, identify the strongest source, and record a verdict with the reasoning.

All cases use public information and are written in my own words. No client or confidential material is included.

## Cases

| # | Claim | Verdict | Finding | Main source |
|---|-------|---------|---------|-------------|
| 1 | The Great Wall of China is visible from space with the naked eye | False | NASA says it is not visible from the Moon and generally not visible unaided from low Earth orbit. | NASA (2005) |
| 2 | Humans use only 10% of their brains | False | Neurologists say nearly all of the brain is used and most of it is active most of the time. | Scientific American |
| 3 | Lightning never strikes the same place twice | False | The National Weather Service says lightning often strikes the same place repeatedly, especially tall structures. | National Weather Service |
| 4 | Einstein failed math at school | False | Einstein rejected the story in 1935, and biographers report strong early results in math. | TIME, Britannica |
| 5 | Napoleon was unusually short | False | His recorded 5 ft 2 in was in French units, which is about 5 ft 6 in today, average for his time. | Britannica, History.com |
| 6 | Honey never spoils | Mostly true | Sealed honey resists spoilage, but it can spoil if water is added or it is stored open in humid air. | Smithsonian Magazine |

## Method

1. Split each claim into parts that can be checked separately.
2. Check each part against independent sources.
3. Identify the primary source, or state that none was found.
4. Record a verdict: True, Mostly true, Misleading, False, or Unverifiable.
5. Note any conflicts between sources and any limits of the check.
